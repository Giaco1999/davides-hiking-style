    Design choices

    Trails

    • Red is reserved for the trail network and the points along it (huts, bivouacs, viewpoints).
    • One red, two dash patterns: dashed for marked trails, dotted for alpine routes (OSM sac_scale T4–T6).
    • Via ferrata: big red dots on a white band, carabiner icons along the line and the words “Via ferrata” written along it from z12.
    • National long-distance trails: thin red line z7–10, pale band under the trail from z11.

    Roads and lines

    • Roads: motorways pink, state roads orange, provincial roads yellow, the rest white and narrow.
    • Forest roads: double line with a tan centre from z12, drawn under the roads. Unmarked paths: brown dash.
    • Railways: solid black. Tunnels: pale fill with dashed black edges.

    Relief

    • Hillshade lit from the north-west, lighter at small scales.
    • Contours: index lines heavier, every 100 m from z14; blue on glaciers, with a firm blue glacier edge.

    Areas

    • Woods from z5 (Planet v4 below z8).
    • Built-up tint from z7; buildings merged into masses z12–14, footprints from z14.
    • Protected areas from z10: purple edge, translucent inner band, name along the boundary. Natura 2000 sites excluded.

    Labels

    • Lakes are a key landmark for reading a territory, especially in the overview at small scales: one horizontal name per lake, sized by area, with the highest label priority.
    • Peaks: shown by rank and by height (over 2,700 m from z8, 2,400 m from z10, 2,000 m from z11), all from z12; higher peaks win. Peak names take priority over the trails around them, so summits reached by a trail always keep their name.
    • Custom icons: huts from z11, bivouacs from z12, viewpoints and caves from z13. Their names avoid trails, using invisible collision symbols along them.
    • Country and region names only in the overview (z5–9), in wide-spaced grey capitals.
    • Trail numbers and river names sit above their line.

    Where to look

    • Monte Amariana (near Tolmezzo): via ferrata.
    • Tre Cime di Lavaredo: huts, trails, trail numbers, lakes.
    • Sorapiss: bivouacs, alpine routes, Lago di Sorapiss.
    • Marmolada: glacier edge and contours.
    • Udine: built-up masses, state roads, railways.
    • The Dolomites and Lake Garda: the overview.
