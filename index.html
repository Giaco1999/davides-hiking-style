    Design choices

    Trails

    • Red is reserved for the trail network and the points along it (huts, bivouacs, viewpoints).
    • One crimson, as on printed hiking maps: all marked trails in long thick dashes, no halo.
    • Via ferrata: red line with cross-ticks, with “Via ferrata” written along it from z12.
    • National long-distance trails: thin red line z7–10, pale band under the trail from z11.

    Roads and lines

    • Roads: motorways pink, state roads orange, provincial roads yellow, the rest white; every road has a dark edge, like the forest roads.
    • Forest roads: double line with a tan centre from z12, drawn under the roads. Unmarked paths: thin black dash.
    • Cable cars and lifts: one cabin icon at the middle of each line, from z12.
    • Railways (Planet v4, from z7): solid black for running lines, thin grey for yards and sidings. Tunnels: pale fill with dashed black edges.

    Relief

    • Hillshade lit from the north-west, lighter at small scales.
    • Contours: all lines from z12; index lines heavier, every 100 m from z14; blue on glaciers.

    Areas

    • Woods from z5 (Planet v4 below z8).
    • Built-up tint from z7; buildings merged into masses z12–14, footprints from z14.
    • Protected areas from z10: thin purple edge and name along the boundary. Natura 2000 sites excluded.

    Labels

    • Lakes are a key landmark for reading a territory, especially in the overview at small scales: one horizontal name per lake, sized by area, with the highest label priority.
    • Peaks: shown by rank and by height (over 2,700 m from z8, 2,400 m from z10, 2,000 m from z11), all from z12; higher peaks win, and names grow with height (under 1,500 m, 1,500–2,500, 2,500–3,000, over 3,000 m). Peak names take priority over the trails around them, so summits reached by a trail always keep their name.
    • Country names at z6–8 in wide-spaced grey capitals, placed by hand in open areas of the map.
    • Custom icons: huts and bivouacs from z12 (Planet v4), other mountain shelters from z14, caves from z13, viewpoints from z15. Their names avoid trails, using invisible collision symbols along them.
    • Trail numbers and river names sit above their line.
